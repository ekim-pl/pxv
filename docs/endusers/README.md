# Your phone: security and daily use

Start here if you receive or use a Pixel prepared with pxv. You do not need a
development computer or CLI. You need your phone’s actual handoff packet, its
profile list, the operator’s real support contact, and private access to your
ordinary unlock and account recovery information.

## Before daily use

1. Follow [receiving your phone](receiving-your-phone.md) with the operator. Match
   the label, check each promised profile and ordinary unlock, and confirm debugging
   is off before accepting custody.
2. Read [PINs, passwords, and duress](pins-passwords-and-duress.md) before entering
   a supplied credential. Ordinary unlocks and destructive credentials have different
   purposes. Never trigger erasure as a test.
3. Review [security and privacy](security-and-privacy.md), then
   [daily use](daily-use.md) and [backups, loss, and support](backup-loss-and-support.md).

Keep the [phone-sized quick start](../../guides/source/markdown/quickstart-en.md)
with the agreed general instructions. A [Polish edition](../../guides/source/markdown/quickstart-pl.md)
is available. Store completed credential inserts securely apart from the phone,
with destructive pages separately sealed.

If you prepare your own phone, also follow the operator’s
[single-device path](../operators/single-device.md). You still perform both operator
and recipient checks. A general guide or an old test result does not establish
that your actual phone passed its requirements.
